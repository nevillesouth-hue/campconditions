Everything to check before you drive to a camp this summer, on one page. Every link goes to an official source.

CampConditions.au is an independent site, not an official warning service. Always check the official sources below before you travel and follow their advice. In an emergency call 000.

1. The 5-minute check before you leave

  1. Is the camp open? Look it up on campconditions.au, then confirm with the park or land manager. “Check” or no notice does not mean open.
  2. Warnings near you? Your state’s warnings site or app (row 1 below), and the Bureau of Meteorology forecast.
  3. Fire danger and bans today, and what you can light? Rows 2 and 3. Check again on the day; bans can be declared overnight.
  4. Park alerts and roads. Rows 4 and 5. Closures for burns, storms, floods and works change daily.
  5. Booked or permit needed? Row 6. Free and council camps: check the council or land manager’s page.

Emergency: 000 in every state. Deaf, hard of hearing or speech impaired: use the National Relay Service and ask for the number. Get the official app for your state (VicEmergency, Hazards Near Me, Alert SA) before you lose signal.

3. Summer safety

  • Heat and water: carry more drinking water than you think; rest in the shade in the hottest hours.
  • Snakes and wildlife: wear boots, use a torch at night, leave snakes alone; know basic snakebite first aid.
  • Patchy mobile coverage: download offline maps and official apps before you go; tell someone your plan and return time.
  • Plan-B exit: know a second way out and a back-up camp; leave early if fire danger is Extreme or Catastrophic.
  • Leave no trace: take all rubbish out, use toilets or bury waste well away from water, stick to existing tracks and sites.

4. Closures that catch people out

Seasonal road and track shutdowns, planned burns, storm damage, flooding and works. Maps and “reopens on” dates can lag behind the latest park alert, so check on the day.

5. Pack check: food, water and clothing

  • Extra meals and drinking water in case of a delayed exit or a closed road
  • Food stored heat-safe (cooler or shade) and wildlife-safe (sealed, locked away, never left out overnight)
  • Sun protection: hat, sunscreen, sunglasses, plus light long-sleeved layers
  • Something warm for cool nights (Tasmania and the High Country can be cold even in summer)
  • Sturdy closed shoes or boots

For free camps, also pack

  • Rubbish bags and a toilet kit (many free camps have no bins or toilets)
  • Charged phone, power bank, paper map and a first aid kit
